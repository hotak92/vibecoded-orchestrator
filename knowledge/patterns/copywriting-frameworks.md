# Copywriting Frameworks for Sales and Marketing

Copywriting frameworks are not paint-by-numbers. They are skeletons you fill with the prospect's actual language. Use the wrong framework for the context and good copy reads as gimmicky. Use the right one and a junior writer produces clear, persuasive copy on the first draft.

## When to use which framework (the decision)

| Context                            | Best framework        |
|------------------------------------|-----------------------|
| Cold outbound email                | PAS, or 3B (Brevity/Blunt/Basic) |
| Landing page hero                  | StoryBrand, or PAS    |
| Long-form sales page               | StoryBrand, AIDA      |
| Ad copy (FB / IG / Google)         | AIDA, BAB             |
| Social post (LinkedIn / X)         | 4Ps, BAB              |
| Webinar / sales-deck narrative     | StoryBrand            |
| Re-engagement email                | BAB                   |
| Product launch announcement        | FAB, PAS              |
| Case study                         | STAR, BAB             |

## The frameworks

### PAS — Problem · Agitate · Solution

The most-used framework in cold outbound and direct response. Surfaces the pain, amplifies what happens if it's unsolved, presents your offer as the relief.

```
Problem:    State the prospect's pain in their words
Agitate:    Make the cost of inaction visible (what breaks, what's lost, who notices)
Solution:   Position your offer as the resolution, with proof
```

**Cold email example**:
> P: Most series-B SaaS founders we talk to are still attributing pipeline manually in spreadsheets.
> A: That's fine at 10 deals/month. At 50, the spreadsheet lies — and your CFO finds out at board prep.
> S: [Tool] auto-attributes across HubSpot + Stripe + Mixpanel in 20 minutes of setup. 2-min demo?

When to skip: PAS is overdone in cold email. If your ICP gets 100 cold emails a week and 80 of them are PAS, switch to BAB or a question-first opener.

### AIDA — Attention · Interest · Desire · Action

Linear funnel framework, oldest of the bunch (1898). Best for ads and long-form pages where you control the full reading flow.

```
Attention:   Hook (headline, image, opening line)
Interest:    Specific, unexpected detail that earns the next 5 seconds
Desire:      Paint the after-state — what life looks like with the problem gone
Action:      Single, specific CTA
```

**Landing page hero example**:
> A: "Your CRM is wrong about 23% of your pipeline."
> I: Most go-to-market data is stitched from 4+ tools. Stitches break. We watched 47 SaaS teams lose deals to bad data in Q1.
> D: With clean attribution, founders walk into board meetings knowing exactly which channels print revenue.
> A: [Get the 5-minute audit →]

### BAB — Before · After · Bridge

Pain-light alternative to PAS. Works when prospect is already aware of the problem (you don't need to agitate) and just needs to see the after-state and how to get there.

```
Before:   Their current state (acknowledged, not over-dramatized)
After:    The transformed state (specific, sensory)
Bridge:   How your offer gets them from Before → After
```

**LinkedIn post example**:
> B: 3 months ago we spent 4 hours/week reconciling Stripe + HubSpot manually.
> A: This morning, we opened HubSpot and revenue attribution was already done.
> B: We wrote a 47-line script. [Link in comments]

### 4Ps — Promise · Picture · Proof · Push

Underrated framework for short-form social and ads. Heavier on proof than the others.

```
Promise:   The outcome (one sentence)
Picture:   Show what the outcome looks like (image, screenshot, story)
Proof:     Data, testimonial, before/after metric
Push:      Soft or hard CTA
```

**X post example**:
> P: You can clean a 10K-row email list in 8 minutes.
> Pic: [screenshot of NeverBounce dashboard]
> Pf: We did it Tuesday — saved 12% in bounce rate, gained 800 opens that week.
> Ph: Reply "list" if you want the exact prompt I used.

### StoryBrand (Donald Miller) — 7-part Hero's Journey for marketing

The framework that earned Miller a $50M business teaching it to other businesses. Best for landing pages, sales pages, and brand messaging. Most powerful when you map the entire brand to it (not just one page).

```
1. Hero (customer, not you)         — they want something
2. Problem (external + internal + philosophical)
3. Guide (you — empathetic + authoritative, NOT the hero)
4. Plan (3-step, simple, branded)
5. Call to Action (direct + transitional)
6. Avoiding failure (what's lost if they don't act)
7. Achieving success (the after-state)
```

**SaaS landing page applied**:
> Hero: Solo founders building B2B SaaS
> Problem: External — pipeline is a mess. Internal — feel like they're guessing. Philosophical — founders should focus on customers, not spreadsheets.
> Guide: "We've helped 320 founders fix this. Here's how."
> Plan: 1. Connect HubSpot 2. We build the dashboard 3. You make decisions
> Direct CTA: "Book a 15-min audit"
> Transitional CTA: "Get the 2-page guide"
> Failure: "Keep guessing where your revenue is coming from"
> Success: "Walk into board meetings with the actual numbers"

The non-obvious move in StoryBrand: **never make yourself the hero.** Your CEO photo on the landing page is not "look at me", it's "I am the guide." Yoda, not Luke.

### FAB — Features · Advantages · Benefits

For product-launch and feature-announcement copy. The discipline: every feature must be translated to an advantage AND a benefit. Vendors who stop at "advantage" never close.

```
Feature:    What the product has (objectively)
Advantage:  Why that's better than the alternative
Benefit:    What that means for the prospect's life/job
```

**Example — a new bulk-edit feature**:
> F: Bulk-edit up to 500 records at once with conditional rules
> A: 10x faster than competitors that cap at 50, no manual spreadsheet exports
> B: Your ops team gets 4 hours back per week. That's an extra customer call per day.

### STAR — Situation · Task · Action · Result

For case studies and testimonials. The structure recruiters use for interview questions also works for evidence-based copy.

```
Situation:  Context (company, scale, state when they started)
Task:       What they were trying to do
Action:     What they did (with you)
Result:     Quantified outcome (with timeframe)
```

Case studies that lead with the Result and then unpack STAR backwards (modern variant) outperform straight STAR for landing pages — readers see the number first.

### 3B (Brevity · Bluntness · Basic) — for cold email specifically

Less a framework, more a discipline. Cold email under 75 words, no buzzwords, one specific ask. From the school of Josh Braun, Jason Bay, Will Allred.

```
Brevity:    < 75 words, prefer < 50
Bluntness:  Say what you want; no "I hope this finds you well"
Basic:      6th-grade reading level (use hemingwayapp.com or readability.io)
```

**Example**:
> "Hey Maria — saw [Company] just hired a Marketing Ops Lead. The first thing most of them rip out is the Mixpanel-HubSpot Zapier mess. We made a one-pager on the 3 setups that don't break. Want me to send it?"

42 words, no fluff, one specific ask, names a real pain.

## The meta-skill: voice over framework

Frameworks are scaffolding. The persuasive part is using the prospect's exact language — words from their job posting, their podcast appearances, their last X post, their support tickets. Tools like Gong and Chorus (call recording with AI transcription) make this trivially easy in B2B.

A vendor whose copy still feels "AI-written" in 2026 isn't using AI badly — they're using frameworks without filling in the voice. Frameworks + AI = same-sounding output. Frameworks + voice-mined-from-calls + AI = the differentiation.

## Headline patterns (use as primer, not crutch)

- **Specific number + payoff**: "The 7-line cold email that books 14% of replies"
- **Contrarian claim + proof**: "Why we killed our 1,200-contact email list (and grew revenue 40%)"
- **Question that names the pain**: "Still using spreadsheets to attribute pipeline?"
- **How to without dropping cost**: "How to clean a 10K-row list in 8 minutes"
- **Negative outcome avoided**: "Stop losing deals to bad CRM data"

## Anti-patterns (avoid)

- ❌ Stacking 3 frameworks in one email (PAS + StoryBrand + FAB = unreadable)
- ❌ Generic "I hope this finds you well" / "I noticed you're a [role]"
- ❌ Listing features without benefits
- ❌ Hero shots of your own product on the hero of the landing page (show outcome, not UI)
- ❌ Long copy that doesn't move (StoryBrand's worst-case implementation is brand-narcissistic)
- ❌ AI-written-from-zero — always start from voice-of-customer data
